# Describing Images

## Giving an Image Context

An image can communicate useful information, but readers may need a written description to understand its purpose. A filename such as photo01.jpg does not explain the subject. A descriptive title, a caption, and accurate metadata help readers interpret the image within a collection.

The elements introduced in [[resource-description/metadata-basics|Metadata Basics]] provide a starting point. Record the creator when known, describe the visible subject, and distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions.

## Writing Different Descriptions

### Title, Caption, and Alternative Text

These descriptions serve different purposes:

- *Title:* Gives the image a short, recognizable name.
- *Caption:* Provides visible context alongside the image.
- *Alternative text:* Communicates the image's relevant information when someone cannot see it.

For an illustrative photograph of soccer supporters, a title could be “Jordan Supporters in the Stands.” A caption could explain the occasion if it is verified. Alternative text could describe supporters holding Jordanian flags, if that is what the photograph shows.

Avoid guessing people's identities, emotions, or the event location. Describe what the image supports and what matters to the surrounding explanation.

## Connecting Images to Their Records

Useful [[resource-description/tags-and-keywords|Tags and Keywords]] can group photographs by subject. However, keywords alone cannot explain the relationships between people, objects, and activities in a scene. A sentence can communicate those relationships more clearly.

Record image credits and permission information separately from the visual description. Finding an image online does not establish permission to reuse it.

## Checking the Published Image

After publishing, confirm that the image loads, its caption is readable, and its alternative text communicates its purpose. These checks belong in the broader review described in [[resource-description/metadata-quality|Metadata Quality]].
